Privacy policy

Effective 2 November 2026 · applies to the Unlose app for Android (com.unlose.app), made by Mulhimfy.

In short

Unlose collects no personal data. It has no internet permission, so nothing it reads can leave your phone through it. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no advertising ID.

What the app reads, and why

  • Files on your phone's shared storage. To find deleted photos, videos, audio and documents, Unlose reads recycle bins, thumbnail caches, hidden folders and cache files on your device and on inserted SD cards. This needs Android's "All files access" permission on Android 11 and later, or the storage permissions on Android 10 and earlier. Unlose asks for it only to show you what can be recovered.
  • Reading happens entirely on your device. Previews are made on the device and kept only in the app's memory and private cache.

What the app writes

  • When you choose to recover files, Unlose saves new copies into folders named "Unlose" (DCIM/Unlose for photos and videos, Music/Unlose for audio, Documents/Unlose for other files). It never edits or deletes the files it finds.
  • "Put back" asks Android to take items out of its own trash; Android shows its confirmation first.
  • In "Restored files", you can delete copies Unlose made, after a confirmation.
  • Your settings (theme, scan options) are stored in the app's private storage on your device. App backup is turned off.

Data shared with others

None. Unlose does not sell, share or transfer any data. It contains no third-party analytics, advertising or tracking libraries. If you choose to share a recovered file, Android's share sheet sends it to the app you pick.

Children

Unlose is a general utility and is not directed at children under 13.

Deleting your data

Unlose keeps no data about you. Uninstalling the app removes its settings and cache. Files you recovered stay in the Unlose folders until you delete them.
